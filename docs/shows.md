# The Shows — every format, every night

> **Status: operating document.** The rundown bible. [`show-bible.md`](show-bible.md) owns
> the room — the clock, the rig, the OBS scene. **This owns the content.**
>
> **The job of this document:** you never face a blank. Eight hours a night is ~208 hours of
> programming between now and Week 0. Nobody improvises that. What makes it survivable is a
> fixed rundown plus a segment library deep enough to pull from cold, on the worst night of
> the month, with no prep.
>
> Graphics called out in `CAPS` are modes of the package in
> [`design/graphics-package.md`](design/graphics-package.md).

| **2:00** | **LAST CALL** | Low | Ritual |

### 1.1 The clip beats — the most important three minutes of the night

**7:20 · 11:15 · 1:50.** Two clean minutes each. One opinion. To camera. Graphic behind.
Delivered knowing it will be cut.

> **Three beats a night is three reels tomorrow, pulled from the broadcast, with no separate
> shoot.**

This is the entire reel engine. It is the difference between "post 3 reels a day" being a
second full-time job and being a byproduct of a show you were doing anyway. **Design the
show so the clips fall out of it**, and the content problem solves itself.

Rules for a clip beat:
- **Say the take in the first sentence.** Not the setup, not the context — the take. A reel
  is won or lost in 1.5 seconds.
- **One idea.** If it needs two, it is two beats on two nights.
- **Land it clean.** No "anyway", no trailing off. The last sentence is the caption.
- **Mark it.** Say the segment name out loud so the timestamp is findable in the VOD.

## 2. THE OPEN — 7:00 PM

The flagship. If someone watches one hour of CSGN ever, it is this one, and it has to be
the best hour on the channel by a distance.

| | Beat | Length | Graphic |
|---|---|---|---|
| :00 | **COLD OPEN** — one line, highest energy of the night. No housekeeping, no "what's up guys" | 90s | Full-frame title |
| :02 | **THE BOARD** — what's coming tonight, who's booked | 3m | `RUNDOWN` |
| :05 | **THE TAPE** — the day's top three stories, ~5m each | 15m | `MATCHUP` · `STAT` |
| :20 | **THE TAKE** ← **CLIP** | 2m | `TAKE` |
| :25 | **THE NUMBER** — one stat that reframes a debate | 7m | `STAT` |
| :32 | **OVERREACTION OR CORRECT** | 13m | `POLL` |
| :45 | **THE RECEIPT** ← **CLIP** | 8m | `TAKE` + quote |
| :55 | **TEASE** — who's on at 8, one sentence, out | 5m | `RUNDOWN` |

**The cold open is the whole show's first impression and it is worth over-preparing.** Write
the first line down. It is the only scripted thing in eight hours.

## 3. The rest of the night

**9:00 THE GAME** — CFB 27 dynasty, run as a season with stakes. Low talk energy, high
watch-time, and it is the hour where the chat becomes a community rather than an audience.
The dynasty is the point: a season people follow beats a game people watch.

**10:00 THE DRAFT** — the community hour. The 30-minute draft on air, then a whip-around of
whoever's live. This is where drafted streamers and Charter contributors get their airtime,
and where the network stops being one person.

**11:00 LATE OPEN** — a genuine reset for the West Coast, not a recap. New arrivals should
never feel they missed the show. Second **CLIP** beat at :15.

**12:00 CO-VIEW** — react to something. Lowest effort per minute of any hour on the clock,
which is exactly why it sits where it does.

**1:00 THE ROOM** — open mics, callers, degen hours. The audience carries it.

**2:00 LAST CALL** — tomorrow's board, the credits roll, sign-off at 3:00 sharp. Third
**CLIP** beat at :50. Ending at the same minute every night is a promise, and promises are
what make a schedule worth anything.

## 4. The 8 PM rotation

| Night | Show | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | **MAILBAG** | Audience and holder questions. No booking required, and it reliably produces the week's best clips because the questions are better than your prompts |
| **Tue · Wed · Thu** | **THE INTERVIEW** | One guest, one hour |
| **Fri** | **FILM ROOM** | Tape review of the week's takes — yours and other people's, scored honestly |
| **Sat** | **THE SLATE** *(from Aug 29)* | Game-day wrap. Before Week 0: extended **THE ROOM** |
| **Sun** | **CO-VIEW** | Preseason, then real football |

**Three interviews a week, never seven.** Nightly booking is the first thing that collapses,
and a no-show guest is a dead hour in primetime. Monday and Friday exist precisely because
they need nobody's calendar but yours.

### 4.1 Booking, briefly

Pitch the slot, not the show: *"One hour, 8 PM ET, on a 24/7 network with a ticker and a
ratings book. Your call record goes on screen. I'll send you the clip."* Ask for a 45-minute
commitment and end at 55. Send the clip within 24 hours, every time — the clip is the fee,
and it is why the second guest says yes.

## 5. The segment library

Twelve formats. Each one can be run cold, with no prep, on any night. **This is the
anti-blank insurance** — when the rundown falls apart at 12:40 AM, you pull from here.

**THE TAKE** — 2 minutes, one opinion, no hedging. *"[Team/player] is [claim]. Here's why."*
The clip beat format.

**THE RECEIPT** — someone's bad take, then what actually happened. Quote on screen, date on
it, outcome next to it. **The information-battle weapon**, and the most satisfying two
minutes in sports media.

**THE NUMBER** — one statistic that reframes an argument everyone is having wrong. Big on
screen, ten seconds of setup, then the number.

**OVERREACTION OR CORRECT** — run last night's loudest reactions past the audience. `POLL`
on screen, live tally, you argue the unpopular side.

**TIER LIST** — S through F. **The most-argued-with format in sports** and the single best
ranking-reel generator. People will comment to disagree with tier placements they don't
care about.

**WHO YA GOT** — head to head, pick one, defend it in thirty seconds. Rapid-fire.

**HEAT CHECK** — rate a circulating take 1–10 on how insane it is. Fast, funny, endlessly
repeatable.

**THE SPREAD** — the line and the total, **as market data only**. What the market thinks and
why. **No picks, no units, no lock of the day** — the graphics carry the number, never a
recommendation.

**CALL IT** — predictions, logged, with a **published win-loss record** that you update in
public whether it's good or bad. See §6.

**THE HOLDER QUESTION** — questions submitted by holders, weighted by supply share
([`design/the-grid.md`](design/the-grid.md) §12.1). The guest is interviewed by the market.

**SIGNING DAY** — welcome new Charter contributors by name, on air, with the counter. Thirty
seconds, and it recruits the next one.

**FILM ROOM** — actual tape. Slow, specific, and the format that most separates you from
people shouting into a webcam.

## 6. Winning the information battle

The stated goal is to beat sports accounts with terrible takes. That is not won by having
better opinions — everyone thinks their opinions are better. It's won mechanically:

### 6.1 Never dunk without a number

The reason bad takes win is that nobody brings evidence, so volume decides. `THE NUMBER` and
`THE RECEIPT` exist so that every disagreement arrives with a fact attached. A take with a
stat on screen beats a louder take without one, and it is far more clippable — the graphic
*is* the hook.

### 6.2 Keep the record in public

`CALL IT` publishes a running win-loss on your own predictions, updated whether it flatters
you or not.

**Almost nobody in sports media does this**, because it is genuinely uncomfortable. It is
also the only thing that compounds into authority: a year of published, mediocre-but-honest
predictions is worth more than a year of confident unrecorded ones. Publish the bad months
first — a record that only starts once the numbers are good is a record nobody believes.

### 6.3 Be the scoreboard, not another voice

Rankings and standings get **cited**. Opinions get scrolled past. The `TOP25` board, the
Book, the Caller Standings — these are the assets other people screenshot, and every
screenshot carries the brand into a room you weren't in.

**The day a competitor screenshots their own row to brag about it, you stopped being a
participant and became the record.**

### 6.4 Be wrong out loud

When a take ages badly, run it as a `RECEIPT` against yourself. It costs one segment and it
buys the thing nobody else in this category has, which is a reason to believe you when you
are right. It is also, reliably, better content than being right.

## 7. What the shows refuse

- **No "what's up guys."** The cold open is a line, not a greeting.
- **No picks sold as analysis.** Lines are context. If it sounds like advice, cut it.
- **No dunking on non-public people.** Big accounts and public figures are fair game;
  someone with 400 followers is not.
- **No dead air waiting for a guest.** If they're five minutes late, start without them and
  bring them in when they arrive.
- **No segment that needs a graphic that doesn't exist yet.** Run the version that works
  tonight; the package catches up.
