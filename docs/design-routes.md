# Front-page design routes — pick one

**What this is.** Three complete creative routes for csgn.fun, presented the way
an agency presents them: each one is a whole position, not a colour swatch. They
are mutually exclusive on purpose. Picking two gets you neither.

**What is already built.** The front page as it stands today is a *neutral*
build — a live stage, a conversion block with three ordered paths, a proof strip
of live facts. It works, it converts, and it commits to nothing. It is the
canvas each route below paints on, not a fourth option.

**How to answer.** Reply with one name. Everything downstream — type, motion,
photography, the tone of every button on the site — follows from that one word.

---

## The brief, restated

CSGN is a 24-hour television channel that anybody can get on. Two audiences
arrive on the same page and want opposite things:

- **The uploader** (the free zone). Has a clip. Wants it seen. Will give you
  eleven seconds and one tap. Cost of entry must round to zero.
- **The block claimer** (the effort zone). Will stream live for two hours and
  earn 30% of the fees. This is a real commitment and the page has to make it
  feel like one — earned, not free.

A third audience reads the page without ever clicking: **brands and projects**
who buy airtime. They are not converted by a form. They are converted by the
site looking like it belongs to a network that already sells things.

The conversion job is to make the uploader's path feel free and the claimer's
path feel earned **on the same screen**, without either one cheapening the other.

---

## Route 1 — DRAPER

> *"Television, sold like television."*

**The position.** CSGN is a broadcast network. Not a crypto site with video on
it — a network, with a schedule, a rate card, and a channel ident. Everything on
the page behaves like something that goes to air.

**How it looks.** Confident and mostly empty. Enormous display type in Space
Grotesk, set tight and left-aligned, on near-black. One accent — the existing
`#ff2346` — used sparingly enough that it still means something when it appears.
The schedule is treated as the hero asset: a real programming grid, hours down
the left, names in the cells, the current hour lit. Photography is the stream
itself; there is no stock imagery anywhere.

**The motion.** Broadcast grammar only. Wipes between slots (already built),
lower thirds that slide in and hold, a clock that ticks. Nothing bounces,
nothing floats, nothing parallaxes. Motion means *something changed on air*.

**The words.** Short declaratives, present tense, no exclamation marks.
"Tonight at 9. Open." "Nobody is on this hour." The channel is stating facts
about itself, which is exactly the register a network uses.

**What it costs you.** Restraint is expensive to hold. This route dies the
moment somebody adds a glowing gradient card because a page felt empty. It also
under-sells to a crypto-native audience who reads calm as low-energy.

**What it buys you.** It is the only one of the three that a brand can put in a
media plan without embarrassment. If selling sponsored blocks and ticker
placement matters within six months, this route is worth more than the other two
combined.

**Fits if:** the near-term revenue is partnerships and the long-term identity is
"a real channel."

---

## Route 2 — BETTY

> *"Everybody's already inside. You're the one standing outside."*

**The position.** CSGN is a scene. The product is the people on it, and the page
is the party you can see through the window. Social proof is the entire design
system.

**How it looks.** Dense, alive, faces everywhere. Avatars in the schedule cells,
the clip reel visible as a live wall of thumbnails on the front page, a
who's-on-next rail that never stops moving. Warmer than the current build —
still dark, but lifted with real colour pulled from the clips themselves rather
than a fixed palette. Cards have edges you can see; the page reads busy on
purpose.

**The motion.** Constant, small, ambient. The clip wall shuffles. The live dot
pulses. Counts tick up. Nothing demands attention individually; the *aggregate*
says this place is populated.

**The words.** Second person and social. "You're on at 9." "Three people took
blocks in the last hour." Every number on the page is other people doing the
thing you have not done yet.

**What it costs you.** This route is a promise that the network is busy, and it
is checkable — an empty clip wall reads as a ghost town far worse than restraint
would. It needs a real floor of activity before it goes up, and it needs
moderation, because your front page becomes user-generated.

**What it buys you.** It is the strongest of the three at converting the free
zone. Somebody who came to watch one clip sees fifty people who posted theirs
and posts one. That is the flywheel, drawn.

**Fits if:** the near-term priority is upload volume and there is enough content
on the wall by launch to make it true.

---

## Route 3 — PEGGY

> *"Here is exactly how it works. Do the math yourself."*

**The position.** CSGN is a system with published rules, and the rules are the
sell. No mystique, no scene, no network glamour — a machine you can audit and
therefore trust with your time.

**How it looks.** Editorial and instrumented. Mono numerals everywhere
(JetBrains Mono is already loaded), tabular figures, real charts. The 30% fee
split, your seconds of airtime versus your holdings, the treasury balance, the
schedule fill rate — all on the front page as live readouts. Diagrams over
photographs. The palette narrows almost to greyscale so the green
(`--color-live`) and red numbers carry all the meaning.

**The motion.** Almost none, and only where a value updated. A number changing
is the animation.

**The words.** Precise and unhedged. "Two-hour blocks. 30% of fees generated
while you are on air, paid in SOL." "Your airtime is your share of supply,
one-to-one." Every claim is a number somebody can check against the chain.

**What it costs you.** It converts the informed and bounces the casual. A TikTok
poster who wants their clip on TV does not want a dashboard, and this route
risks pushing the free zone away at exactly the moment it should be frictionless.

**What it buys you.** Enormous credibility with holders and with anybody who has
been rugged before — which, in this market, is everybody. It is also the most
defensible route: you cannot be accused of over-promising by a page that only
prints measurements.

**Fits if:** the token is the centre of gravity and the audience you most need to
convince is a sceptical one.

---

## The recommendation

**DRAPER**, with one borrowed device from BETTY.

The reasoning is not aesthetic. Two of your three audiences — claimers and
brands — are converted by the site looking like a network that already exists,
and DRAPER is the only route that does that. It also happens to be the cheapest
to hold as the product grows, because restraint scales and density does not.

The borrow: put BETTY's live clip wall in one strip under the stage. It is the
single highest-converting element in any of the three routes, and one strip of
it inside a restrained page reads as *proof*, whereas a whole page of it reads
as *noise*.

PEGGY's readouts are not lost — they belong on /participate and /schedule, where
somebody has already decided to care. They are the second page, not the first.

## What actually changes, per route

| | DRAPER | BETTY | PEGGY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | Programming grid | Live clip wall | Live numbers |
| Type | Huge display, tight | Mid-weight, dense | Mono numerals |
| Colour | Near-black, one accent | Warm, clip-derived | Near-greyscale + signal |
| Motion | Broadcast wipes only | Constant ambient | Value changes only |
| Primary CTA | "Take a block" | "Post yours" | "See the split" |
| Risk | Reads as quiet | Reads as empty if empty | Reads as cold |
| Best for | Partnerships | Upload volume | Holder trust |

## Decisions I need from you

1. **One name.** DRAPER, BETTY, PEGGY — or DRAPER + the clip-wall borrow, which
   is what I would build.
2. **Which CTA is primary above the fold** — "post a clip" (volume) or "claim a
   block" (value). Today it is post; the route may want to change that.
3. **Whether the partner line stays on the front page.** It is one sentence now.
   DRAPER would justify giving it a proper rate-card page.

Nothing below the fold changes until you answer, and none of these routes
requires re-plumbing anything — the conversion block, the paths and the proof
strip are the same components in all three. This is a surface decision with a
long tail, which is the only kind worth presenting as options.
