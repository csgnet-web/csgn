## 1. The terminology play — read this first

You said you want to own SocialFi, StreamFi, ACM, and novel crypto. **One
correction, and it's the most important strategic call in this document.**

**You cannot own "SocialFi."** It's an established category with incumbents,
funds, and Farcaster sitting at ~3.2M MAU. Fighting for it is a losing frame — you
become "another SocialFi project," which is the exact commodity position to avoid.

**You can own StreamFi and ACM outright.** Both are near-uncontested, both
describe what you actually built, and — this is the point — **SocialFi needs
them.** Own the adjacent term the big category lacks, and the big category starts
citing you. *That* is how you get to the forefront of SocialFi: not by joining it,
by supplying its missing vocabulary.

### 1.1 The canonical definitions — use these verbatim, everywhere

Terms spread when there is exactly one crisp definition to quote. Do not
improvise these. Same words in the bio, the essays, the room, the site.

> **StreamFi** *(n.)* — Live streaming where the **airtime itself is the onchain
> asset**: owned, priced, traded, and settled. Not a stream with a token bolted
> on; a stream whose *time* is the instrument.

> **Attention Capital Markets (ACM)** *(n.)* — Markets where **attention is the
> underlying asset** — allocated by auction, verified by proof, and programmable
> by anyone. Attention stops being something you rent indirectly (ads) and becomes
> something you own directly (airtime).

> **CSGN, in one line** — *Blockspace for attention. A 24/7 channel where the
> hour is the asset.*

### 1.2 The three-sentence talk track (memorize this)

Use it in the room, on podcasts, in DMs. Same three beats every time:

1. *"Every social app tokenized **who you know**. Nobody tokenized **when you're
   watched**."*
2. *"A feed with nobody in it is dead. A channel with nobody in it is still a
   channel — that's why one person can build a network."*
3. *"So we made the hour the asset. That's StreamFi, and it's the first real
   Attention Capital Market."*

### 1.3 Ownership rules

- **Every** artifact carries one term. No exceptions — clip captions, ticker
  headlines, essay titles, replies.
- **Define before you promote.** The definitions above ship on the site and in
  essay #1 *before* the campaign pushes the terms hard. You cannot own a word
  people can't look up.
- **Give the terms away.** Encourage others to use StreamFi/ACM without credit.
  Category creators win on adoption, not attribution — and the citation trail
  leads back to you anyway.
- **Never say "we're the SocialFi project."** Say *"we're StreamFi — the part of
  SocialFi nobody built."*

## 2. Pillar 1 — The Room

**Job:** credibility and borrowed reach with exactly the right audience.
**Owner:** you. **Cadence:** daily, ~1 hour, non-negotiable.

### 2.1 How to be in it

- **Useful before interesting.** Have the number, the chart, the answer. Be the
  person worth having in the room on the merits.
- **Never pitch. Still.** Permission to *stream* is not permission to *sell*.
  The moment you pitch in someone else's room, you spend the credibility that
  makes the room valuable. The product comes up when someone asks — and they will,
  because you'll be the guy broadcasting it.
- **Talk StreamFi and ACM, not CSGN.** Be the theorist, not the founder. Theory is
  quotable and travels; a pitch dies in the room it's said in. This is the single
  biggest behavioral edge available to you.
- **Bring one artifact a day.** A graphic, a standings card, a rendered call. The
  room gets a gift, you get a signature.

### 2.2 The pipeline (this is the machine)

```
Ansem room (live)
   → CSGN broadcast (permissioned forward)
      → VOD (full session, YouTube)
         → 3–5 vertical clips (partner)   → TikTok / Shorts / X
         → 1 quote graphic (partner)      → X / Farcaster, attributed + tagged
         → 1 ticker headline              → on-air
         → 1 Discord drop                 → community
```

**The attribution rule:** every clip and graphic tags the speaker and credits the
room. Always. It's correct, and it makes the people in the room *want* you
broadcasting — you're their clip service, for free.

### 2.3 The permission, in writing

Get the streaming permission confirmed **in text** (a DM is fine) covering: what
you may broadcast, attribution, and how anyone opts out of a clip. Not because
anyone's adversarial — because memories differ in six months, and this is the
relationship the whole plan rests on.

## 3. Pillar 2 — The Show (CFB 27)

**Job:** retention, serialization, and the league that makes other people post.
**Owner:** you (host), partner (production).

Three formats, three different jobs. Run all three — but understand what each is
*for*, because that determines cadence and where the output goes.

| Format | Job | Cadence | Primary output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Online 1v1** | Energy + clips | 2–3×/week, short | Verticals (TikTok/Shorts) |
| **Offline hyper-real dynasty** | **Serialization / lore** | 2×/week, ~90 min | VOD + episode arcs |
| **Online dynasty vs. randoms** | **Community + distribution** | Weekly league night | League drama, standings, *other people's posts* |

### 3.1 Why the offline dynasty matters more than it looks

It's the only format that **compounds**. Recruits become characters, rivals
recur, a season is an arc — so a viewer who missed a week has a reason to catch
up, and the VOD library becomes a *show* instead of a pile of recordings. Name the
program (e.g. *"The Dynasty"*), number the episodes, keep a standings graphic on
the ticker. Treat it like television, because you already have the graphics stack.

### 3.2 Why the online dynasty league is the growth asset — start it FIRST

A 12-team league is **11 other people with a reason to post every week.** That is
the highest-leverage distribution asset in the entire plan (and it was already
the #2 odds-mover in [`master-plan.md`](master-plan.md) §12.3). Leagues take weeks
to fill and stabilize, so **recruiting starts week 1**, not after the show is
polished.

Recruit from: the Ansem room, your partner's audience, X replies, Discord. Pitch
it as *"a real league, standings on a broadcast ticker, weekly on CSGN"* — the
production quality is the recruiting hook nobody else can offer.

### 3.3 The crypto bridge — do not skip this

CFB content is the reach; **the bridge is what makes it CSGN's reach and not just
gaming content.** Every session, without fail:

- The ticker runs the whole time (prices, standings, the rail).
- One segment per stream is crypto (a coin spotlight, a market beat, an ACM
  explainer). Two minutes is enough.
- The claim CTA appears every stream: *"the 3pm hour is open — take it."*

Gaming reach that never converts to the network is a hobby. The bridge is what
makes it a funnel.

## 7. On "$10M market cap on attention alone"

Straight answer, because you'd want it: **attention → market cap is real in
crypto, and $10M is not a crazy number for a project with a live channel, a
category it named, and a room like that one behind it.** I'm not going to talk you
out of the ambition.

But three things have to be said plainly:

1. **Market cap is the scoreboard, not the play.** You cannot execute "get to
   $10M." You can execute "manufacture attention reliably and ship the mechanism."
   Cap follows. Teams that target the number directly end up optimizing for
   announcements instead of product, and the market has gotten good at spotting it.
2. **Sequence matters more than size.** If attention arrives *before* the token
   does something structural, you get a spike, a dump, and a reputation — that is
   precisely the Era 1 silhouette ([`socialfi-era2.md`](socialfi-era2.md) §1).
   **Proof-of-Broadcast and airtime-as-an-asset should ship before the peak, not
   after it.** That's why they're in weeks 2–4 above and not "later."
3. **Never put a market-cap target in public.** It reads as a promise, it ages
   badly, and it invites exactly the kind of scrutiny you don't want. Publish the
   mechanism; let the chart be the chart.

**The honest version of the bet:** the attention is winnable — the room, the
partner, the category vocabulary and a real product are a genuinely strong hand.
The risk was never getting noticed; it's **getting noticed with a decorative
token.** Ship the mechanism and the attention has somewhere to land.
